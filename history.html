History of Homeopathy in Canada

Homeopathy has a long and influential history in Canada, beginning in the mid‑19th century and continuing to evolve into a regulated profession in modern times. Below is a chronological overview of key milestones that shaped the development of homeopathic medicine across the country.

1845 – Homeopathy first arrived in Canada when Dr. J.O. Rosenstein, a Dutch immigrant, settled in Montreal.
Ontario became the first province to regulate the practice of homeopathy. Dr. James Lillie and Dr. Joseph Lancaster were the two homeopaths who introduced homeopathy to Ontario.
1854 – The Homeopathic Medical Society of Canada was established, granting homeopaths legal rights to practice.
1859 – “An Act Respecting Homeopathy” was passed. This act set standards for the profession, including a requirement to complete a 3‑year homeopathy program.
1869 – The College of Physicians and Surgeons of Ontario (CPSO) was established, including Homeopaths, Eclectics, and Regular Physicians. Homeopathic medicine remained regulated under CPSO from 1869 to 1974.
1884 – An estimated 80 homeopaths were practicing in Canada.
1890 – The Toronto Homeopathic Hospital opened at Richmond and Duncan Streets.
Emily Stowe, the first female physician in Canada, publicly practiced homeopathic medicine.
1992 – The first homeopathic practitioner program in Canada was introduced by the International Academy of Homeopathy (IAH) and the Toronto Homeopathic Hospital.
Homeopathic remedies became regulated as Natural Health Products in Canada. Health Canada has approved more than 8,500 homeopathic remedies.
2015 – The practice of homeopathy became officially regulated in Ontario by the College of Homeopaths of Ontario (CHO) under the Regulated Health Professions Act (RHPA), effective April 1, 2015.
